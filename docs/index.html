All public datasets, analysed in Python. Each one starts with a business question rather than a technique, and ends with something a manager could act on.

Telecom 7,043 customers

Customer churn prediction

Which customers are about to leave, and what do they have in common?

Contract type dominates everything else. Month-to-month customers churn at 42.7% against 2.8% on two-year terms, and electronic-check payers churn at 45.3%. A Random Forest reached AUC 0.84, with contract length and tenure the strongest predictors.

Retention spend belongs on contract migration and payment-method switching, not on discounts spread thinly across the whole base.

Retail 9,994 transactions

Sales & profitability analysis

Revenue is growing — why isn't profit following?

Discounting past 40% pushes margin negative, so part of the revenue growth was actively destroying value. Technology returned 16.3% margin against Furniture's 8.5%, and 20% of customers generated 60% of revenue.

Cap discount authority at the point margin turns, and rebalance the range toward the categories that actually pay.

Banking 10,000 customers

Customer churn & retention strategy

Is churn a product problem or a market problem?

Both, and they separate cleanly. Attrition ran 32% higher in the German market than elsewhere, while customers holding multiple products retained 40% better. The high-risk profile is consistent: single-product holders with high balances who have gone inactive.

Cross-sell is a retention lever, not just a revenue one — and the German book needs its own diagnosis rather than a global fix.

Human resources 1,470 employees

Employee attrition modelling

Who leaves, and is it something we control?

Overtime carries 2.5× the attrition risk, while higher earners are 3× less likely to leave. The correlation structure is clean: monthly income −0.43, overtime +0.35, years without promotion +0.32. Logistic regression reached 85% accuracy.

Attrition here is a workload and progression problem before it's a pay problem — and both are cheaper to fix than to replace people.

Fintech · risk 0.6% positive class

Credit card fraud detection

How do you model an event that almost never happens?

The hard part is class imbalance, not accuracy — a model predicting "never fraud" would score above 99% and be useless. Strategic undersampling plus RobustScaler for outlier-heavy financial features made the signal usable, with V4 and V11 the strongest indicators.

Tiered alerts scored by risk, so review effort lands where it pays — roughly 3× faster than flat manual review.

Insurance 1,338 policies

Medical cost & risk assessment

What actually drives a premium — who you are, or what you do?

What you do. Smoking status swamps everything else, with smokers paying 3–4× more, while BMI and age act as secondary factors and obesity adds 20–30%. Lifestyle variables consistently outweigh demographic ones.

Preventive-care programmes targeting modifiable behaviour move the cost base more than repricing by age bracket.

Automotive 5,000+ sales records

BMW global sales, 2010–2024

Where is the range actually heading over fifteen years?

Electric and hybrid adoption surged 20% after 2020, with the i-Series holding premium pricing as a halo range. Volume and margin tell different stories: the 5 Series and X3 carry 60% of units, while M-Series commands a 45% price premium.

Electrification strategy and regional allocation should be planned against two separate curves, not one blended one.

Sport 128 players · 107 metrics

Football player performance benchmarking

How do you compare players who do completely different jobs?

With 107 metrics per player, the work is deciding which ones matter by position rather than ranking everyone on one scale. Position-specific benchmarks surfaced Tammy Abraham at 0.5 goals per 90 and Thiago Alcántara at 89.6% pass completion, plus a broader shift toward defenders contributing offensively.

A comparable scouting frame across positions, instead of incomparable raw totals.
