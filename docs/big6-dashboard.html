How the totals are worked out

  • Spend is the sum of transfer and loan fees for incoming players. Free signings, undisclosed fees, loan returns and academy promotions add nothing.
  • 2009/10 to 2022/23 fees are Transfermarkt's, in euros. Later seasons use fees reported in the press, mostly in pounds and often including potential add-ons, converted at the yearly average rate below. January 2026 fees were reported in euros.
  • A season is a summer window plus the January after it. Deals agreed early and completed later, such as Echeverri, Páez, Mamardashvili and Estêvão, count when the player arrived.
  • These are gross figures: money from sales is not taken off.

How the sales figures are worked out

  • Sale income is the sum of transfer and loan fees received. Free transfers, released players, undisclosed fees and loans without a fee add nothing.
  • Fees to 2022/23 are Transfermarkt's, in euros. Later fees are as reported in the press, mostly in pounds and often including potential add-ons, converted at the same yearly rates as the spend tab. January 2026 fees were reported in euros.
  • Net spend is the spend tab's total minus sale income for the same club and season.
  • Fee gain compares each permanent sale with the fee paid for that player's latest permanent signing. Academy graduates, including teenagers signed for youth-team compensation, count as costing nothing. It leaves out wages, agents' fees and sell-on clauses.
  • The accounts figure is a different measure: clubs spread a purchase fee over the contract, so a player bought for €60m and sold for €40m years later can still show a profit.
  • Before 2023/24 every loan out is listed; later round-ups list only first-team loans, so loan counts are not comparable across years. Fee totals are.

How to read them

  • Games are competitive matches only: league, domestic cups (including replays and two-legged semi-finals), the Community Shield, and European and world club competitions. Friendlies and matches awarded without being played are left out. 2026/27 counts games up to 16 October 2026.
  • A season runs from the Community Shield in August to the end of May; the summer Club World Cup counts in the season before it.
  • Revenue follows City's financial year, which ended on 31 May until 2015/16 and on 30 June after that. Deloitte restated the 13-month 2016/17 period to 12 months.
  • Deloitte's figures cover 2009/10 to 2022/23. For 2023/24 and 2024/25 the split comes from City's own accounts, scaled to match Deloitte's euro total. City's 2025/26 accounts are not out yet.
  • Broadcasting includes Premier League TV money and UEFA prize money; it is not only the TV rights deal itself.

How to read them

  • Wage bills are total staff costs from each club's published accounts, in pounds, for the financial year that ends in the summer of the season shown. They include coaches and office staff, bonuses and pay-offs, not only players.
  • Different years come from different summaries of the same accounts, so small differences between sources (a few £m) are possible. City 2016/17 covers 13 months and Liverpool 2011/12 covers 10 months.
  • Not found: Tottenham 2009/10 and 2012/13. Chelsea's 2024/25 accounts do not separate staff costs. 2025/26 is not included because most clubs publish those accounts between November and March.
  • Player wages are estimates from salary-tracking sites (SalaryLeaks, which follows Capology) and press reports. They are basic weekly pay before tax and leave out bonuses and image rights.
  • Manager pay is press-reported and mixes bases: some figures are basic salary, France Football's include bonuses, and one (Moyes) is a contract value divided by its length.
